# Sources

## Policy

Rank sources by how close they are to the fact:

1. **Primary**: electoral authority, official poll notice or the pollster's own release with its tables, the candidate's or party's own documents, statistics office, fiscal council, central bank, parliament.
2. **Independent analysis**: fiscal think tanks, academic work, public broadcasters, wire services (AFP, Reuters, AP, dpa, EFE, ANSA). Use them for costings and context; name them.
3. **Finders only**: aggregators, Wikipedia "opinion polling" pages, newspapers. Use them to discover a poll or a document, then cite the primary source they point to. Wikipedia can be cited only when the claim carries a reliable reference, and then cite that reference too.

Never cite: media with a strongly partisan editorial line (whatever the side; the user's banned list is in `radar.config.json`), content farms, AI-generated aggregator sites, anonymous social media posts. A poll commissioned by a partisan outlet can still be used if its figures come from the official notice or the pollster's own tables: cite those, not the outlet.

When two sources disagree, show both with their dates and say which you retained and why.

## Starting points by country

Verify each at init: institutions and URLs change. For a country not listed, find the equivalents of each line (electoral authority, poll regulator or pollsters' association, statistics office, fiscal council, independent costing), and use the IMF World Economic Outlook and OECD for comparable macro figures.

### France

- Polls: notices filed with the Commission des sondages (commission-des-sondages.fr/notices), one per published poll. No poll may be published the day before and the day of the vote.
- Election: Conseil constitutionnel (sponsorships, official candidate list, results), ministère de l'Intérieur (calendar, results), vie-publique.fr (rules explained).
- Campaign finance: CNCCFP.
- Budget: INSEE (debt, deficit), Cour des comptes, Haut Conseil des finances publiques, Banque de France. Costings: Institut Montaigne, OFCE, Institut des politiques publiques, Fipeco.

### United Kingdom

- Polls: members of the British Polling Council must publish full tables on their own site.
- Election: Electoral Commission (rules, finance), House of Commons Library (results, briefings).
- Budget: ONS, Office for Budget Responsibility. Costings: Institute for Fiscal Studies (manifesto analyses), Resolution Foundation.

### Germany

- Polls: each institute's own release; wahlrecht.de lists them with links (finder).
- Election: Die Bundeswahlleiterin (calendar, admitted parties, results); bpb Wahl-O-Mat for party positions (as a pointer to the programmes).
- Budget: Destatis, Bundesbank, Sachverständigenrat, Stabilitätsrat. Costings: ZEW, ifo, IW, DIW (name the institute; they lean differently on policy).

### United States

- Polls: the pollster's own release with crosstabs; AAPOR Transparency Initiative members disclose methods. Aggregators (finders only).
- Election: FEC (candidates, finance), state election offices (ballot access, results).
- Budget: CBO, Treasury. Costings: Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget, Tax Policy Center, Penn Wharton Budget Model.

### Spain

- Polls: CIS (public) and private institutes' releases. No poll may be published in the five days before the vote.
- Election: Junta Electoral Central, Ministerio del Interior.
- Budget: INE, AIReF, Banco de España. Costings: Fedea, AIReF reports.

### Italy

- Polls: sondaggipoliticoelettorali.it (official register of the Presidency of the Council: full notice for each published poll). No poll may be published in the 15 days before the vote.
- Election: Ministero dell'Interno (Eligendo).
- Budget: ISTAT, Ufficio parlamentare di bilancio, Banca d'Italia. Costings: Osservatorio CPI.

### EU-wide figures

Eurostat (debt and deficit under the common definition), European Commission forecasts and country reports.
